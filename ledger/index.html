Revenue bridge
Decomposes period-on-period revenue change into volume, price, and mix effects. The opening diagnostic on every commercial engagement — answers "why did revenue grow or shrink?" before any deeper analysis begins.
↗
Load your P&L data to build the revenue bridge
Expects monthly financial data with at least date and revenue columns. Richer decomposition when product line and channel are available.
↗
How the revenue bridge is constructed

The bridge decomposes total revenue change (period 2 vs period 1) using: Volume effect = change in units × prior period average price. Price effect = price change × current period units. Mix effect = residual from channel or product composition shift. When only revenue data is available (no unit volume), the bridge estimates directional effects from revenue growth rates and margin movements.

Reading the margin decomposition

Each profit line is expressed as % of revenue per period. The YoY delta column shows whether the margin has expanded (+) or compressed (−). Gross margin declining while EBITDA margin holds means fixed cost leverage is masking a COGS problem. Watch the gap between gross and EBITDA margin: widening gap signals SG&A scale-up; narrowing gap signals cost discipline.

Cost structure analysis
Breaks down operating costs as % of revenue by category. Identifies where cost ratios are expanding relative to revenue growth — the leading indicator of structural inefficiency, typically 2–3 quarters before it becomes a board-level problem.
⊹
Load P&L data with cost breakdown

Interpreting the cost ratio chart

Each bar shows the cost category as % of revenue, averaged across the most recent 6 months vs prior year. Bars above their prior-year level = cost ratio expanding. COGS expansion signals pricing pressure or input cost inflation. G&A expansion signals overhead growth outpacing revenue.

Working capital health
Computes DSO, DPO, DIO, and the cash conversion cycle. Working capital deterioration is one of the earliest signals of financial stress — visible here 2–3 quarters before it appears as a cash flow problem.
⟳
Load balance sheet data
Maps receivables, payables, inventory alongside revenue and COGS for full CCC computation.
⟳
How working capital metrics are computed

DSO = (Receivables ÷ Revenue) × 30 · rising = customers paying slower.
DPO = (Payables ÷ COGS) × 30 · rising = holding supplier payments longer.
DIO = (Inventory ÷ COGS) × 30 · rising = inventory building.
CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO · lower = healthier cash cycle.

Cash conversion cycle trend
CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO · monthly · lower is better

Reading the cash flow waterfall

Starts at EBITDA. Each bar shows: working capital change (+ = WC release / cash in; − = WC build / cash out), capex (always negative), interest + tax (negative). Free cash flow is the final bar. When WC movement is estimated from DSO/DPO/DIO trends, it is marked as an approximation.

How the forecast model works — step by step

Step 1 — Trend: Linear regression through the monthly revenue series. Isolates the underlying growth trajectory.

Step 2 — Seasonal indices: Ratio of actual revenue to trend-expected revenue for each calendar month, averaged across years. Index > 1.0 = above-trend month.

Step 3 — Projection: Extend the trend line forward × seasonal index per month. Bounds = ±1 standard deviation of historical residuals.

Caveat: Linear trend assumes constant growth. Appropriate for stable businesses; will overestimate for decelerating ones.
