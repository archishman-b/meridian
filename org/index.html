Span of control
Direct reports per manager, by level and department. Benchmarked against target spans (IC: not applicable, Manager: 6–8, Director: 4–6, VP: 3–5). Identifies under-spanned layers (management overhead) and over-spanned roles (control risk). Under-span at the director level was the first structural signal in the Renova Org diagnostic.
⊹
Load headcount data to analyse span of control
Expects employee ID, manager ID, level, and department as minimum schema. Manager ID enables the direct report count computation.
⊹
How span of control is computed

For each manager (any employee who appears as a managerId for another employee), we count their direct reports. We then group managers by their own level to produce average, min, and max spans per level. Target spans are: Manager (L3/L4): 6–8 direct reports. Director (L2): 4–6. VP/Head (L1): 3–5. Under-span (< lower bound) = management overhead; over-span (> upper bound) = control risk.

Layer benchmarking
Number of organisational layers, headcount pyramid by level, and cost per layer. Benchmarks against standard spans — most healthy organisations operate with 4–6 layers between CEO and IC. Excess layers add coordination cost and slow decision-making.
▦
Load headcount data with level information
Expects level (numeric or categorical) and optionally salary for cost-per-layer computation.
▦
Reading the layer analysis

The pyramid shows headcount at each organisational level. A healthy pyramid narrows sharply as you move up — the ratio of individual contributors to managers should be 6:1 or higher. A wide upper layer (many managers relative to ICs) signals management overhead. The IC:Manager ratio and spans-of-control work together — low span + wide upper layer is the classic over-managed organisation signature.

Attrition analysis
Voluntary attrition rate by department, level, tenure band, and manager. Identifies where attrition is concentrated, whether it is regrettable (loss of experienced talent) or natural, and how it correlates with structural factors like span and layer. In the Renova Org diagnostic, the store management layer (L3) showed attrition 2.4× the company average.
↗
Load headcount data with attrition information
Expects status (active/left) or an attrition flag column. Exit reason enables voluntary vs involuntary split.
↗
How attrition rate is computed

Attrition rate = Employees who left ÷ Total headcount (leavers + current) × 100%. Computed per group (department, level, tenure band). Voluntary attrition = where exit reason does not indicate performance-managed exit or redundancy. Regrettable attrition = employees with tenure > 2 years who left voluntarily — the highest-cost category.

Industry benchmark for retail (India): 18–22% annual attrition. Store management roles: typically 15–20%. Above 25% in any level is a structural signal warranting investigation.

Attrition heatmap
A two-dimensional grid of attrition intensity — department on one axis, level on the other. Each cell is coloured by attrition rate: dark red = high risk, teal = healthy. The heatmap reveals intersection patterns invisible in aggregate views — a specific level in a specific department can be a hotspot even when both the level average and department average look acceptable.
◎
Load headcount data to build the heatmap
Requires department, level, and status (active/left) for the two-dimensional grid.
◎
Reading the heatmap

Each cell shows attrition rate for the intersection of that department and level. Cells are only shown where there are at least 3 employees (smaller populations produce unreliable rates). Hover over any cell to see headcount and attrition count. The heatmap often reveals that company-level attrition is dominated by 2–3 specific intersections — find and address those, and the aggregate rate improves significantly.

Capability investment mapping
Headcount and salary cost distributed across capability categories — revenue-generating, support, and overhead. Identifies whether headcount growth has been concentrated in value-creating or overhead roles, and whether the investment pattern is aligned with the company's strategic priorities.
⬟
Load headcount data to map capability investment
Department and optionally salary data required. Capability categories are auto-classified from department names and can be overridden.
⬟
How capability categories are assigned

Revenue-generating: Sales, commercial, customer success, store operations, field — roles that directly generate revenue or serve customers. Support: HR, finance, IT, legal, compliance, analytics — roles that enable revenue-generating functions. Overhead: Executive, strategy, admin, corporate — roles that govern the organisation. A benchmark ratio: 65–70% revenue-generating, 20–25% support, 8–12% overhead. Significant deviation signals investment misalignment.
