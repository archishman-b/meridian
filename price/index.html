M3 — Price-Volume Elasticity
How does price move volume?
Estimates price elasticity from transaction data — the relationship between discount level and units sold. Identifies which product categories are elastic (discount-responsive) versus inelastic (quality-anchored).
↗
Load data to compute elasticity
Requires transaction data with discount %, quantity/units, and a product or category dimension.
↗
What the elasticity coefficient means

The coefficient is estimated via Pearson correlation between discount % and units per transaction, scaled by the ratio of their standard deviations. Reading the number: –1.5 means a 1% increase in discount is associated with a 1.5% increase in units sold (elastic — discounting moves volume). –0.4 means a 1% discount increases volume by only 0.4% (inelastic — customers buy regardless of discount). Positive coefficients (rare) suggest discounts are associated with smaller orders — a signal of customer mix effects.

Use the category filter above to isolate elasticity for a specific product line. Different categories in the same business can have very different elasticity profiles.

M5 — Competitor Benchmarking
How do we compare on price?
Compares your pocket prices against floor prices and target prices — derived from your mapped cost and list price data.
How floor and target prices are calculated: Floor price = unit cost × 1.10 (cost plus 10% minimum margin — below this, the deal is structurally unprofitable). Target price = list price × 0.90 (the price at maximum policy discount — pocket prices below this indicate policy breach). Both are computed per-product from your mapped data. If unit cost is not mapped, floor price cannot be computed.

M7 — Deal Scoring
Which deals are structurally sound?
Scores each transaction against five criteria: margin health, price positioning, discount within policy, customer tier alignment, and volume justification. Flags deals that require review.
◇
Load data to score deals
Deal scoring requires price, cost, discount, and ideally customer tier dimensions to be mapped.
◇
Deal scoring — 5 criteria, exact thresholds
1. Margin health (–30/–15 pts)
Margin <20%: –30 pts · Margin 20–35%: –15 pts · Above 35%: no penalty
2. Discount level (–25/–12 pts)
Discount >25%: –25 pts · Discount 15–25%: –12 pts · Below 15%: no penalty
3. Floor price compliance (–25 pts)
Pocket price < unit cost × 1.05: –25 pts (negative-margin deal)
4. Tier alignment (–10 pts)
B2B or SMB customer with discount >20%: –10 pts (below-policy for tier)
5. Extreme outlier (–15 pts)
Discount >30%: additional –15 pts regardless of other criteria
All deals start at 100. Penalties are additive — a deal with 28% discount, 18% margin, and B2B tier loses 25+15+10 = –50 pts → score 50 (Review). Bands: 70–100 = Sound · 40–70 = Review · <40 = Flag
